HEIMDALLR is a public web tool for seeing how the sky changes in NASA's SPHEREx survey. Visitors browse an all-sky view, click any point or type its coordinates, and the tool plays the SPHEREx DR1 images of that patch as a blink animation, with difference and stack views. Frames come only from the same detector at matching wavelengths, because SPHEREx's filters change wavelength across every image, and all sit on a fixed sky grid so real motion is never centered away. Gaia DR3 positions moved to each visit's date separate known stars from anything new.

Machines suggest where to look and people check. A survey of 75,756 random fields compared SPHEREx with 2MASS across 25 years and found 262 known moving stars and 41 unidentified candidates, which wait in a review queue. Anyone can rate a place Moving, Not moving, or Noise or artifact, and the pooled ratings give each candidate a status. Five additional analyses go further, from a 25-year comparison with 2MASS, WISE and NEOWISE to spectra of interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS. Results are always called candidates, and missing data is shown as missing.
